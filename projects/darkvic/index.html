About DarkVic

Power outages have a map. Communication blackouts don't.

When a mobile tower loses mains power, its backup battery keeps it alive for a few hours. Then it goes silent, and that failure also takes out the path that would have reported it. To everyone outside, that silence looks exactly like "no news".

  • Reads mains-power loss from AusNet's public outage feed
  • Tracks hazards and exposure from VicEmergency
  • Reports a probable blackout, never a false certainty

The problem

A dead tower cannot report itself

Power outages are published within minutes. Mobile blackouts aren't published at all. Carriers' battery figures are commercial-in-confidence, and the failure erases its own reporting path.

How it works

Reason from the evidence that survives

A pipeline that runs every 15 minutes. Everything on the map is pre-computed by it, so the dashboard never re-implements the logic.

01

Sense

A Python poller collects AusNet outages and VicEmergency hazards every 15 minutes.

02

Remember

Every snapshot is kept in a versioned event register, so any moment can be replayed.

03

Reason

Explicit rules infer each site's probable state, roll it up to towns and score confidence.

04

Show

A dashboard with two audience views, a replay, the live register and analysis tools.

R1–R5
Site rules

One site's probable state, from mains loss and the time elapsed since.

A1–A5
Aggregation rules

Sites roll up into a suburb or town verdict through their dependency areas.

C1–C4
Confidence rules

How fresh and consistent the evidence is, shown next to every verdict.

The system reports probable blackout, never certainty. No evidence is not the same as safe.

Digital infrastructure

Seven layers, one working system

Every layer of the subject is a working component here, not a proposal.

1

Sensing

A 15-minute Python poller over the AusNet and VicEmergency feeds.

2

Transport

Scheduled HTTPS collection of public feeds into the project archive.

3

Storage

A timestamped snapshot archive plus a cumulative outage event register.

4

Processing

The R1–R5, A1–A5 and C1–C4 state model and the export pipeline.

5

Interface

The dashboard: two audience views, replay, live register, analysis and accessibility.

6

Governance

Visible assumptions, honesty statements, data licensing and AI disclosure.

7

Sustainability

Polite polling of open feeds and additive scaling to all five distributors.

What it means

Two audiences already served. A State Control Centre intelligence view during the event, and an AusNet planning view before it.

Accessible by design. A colour-blind-safe palette, adjustable text size, a guided tour and a plain-language glossary.

Honest limits

One distributor piloted. Eastern Victoria only, by design.

Battery endurance is assumed, not known. The assumption is always on screen.

Inference never carries the authority of observation. This is an educational prototype, not an operational system.

Join the network

What a feed needs

  • The affected area of each outage, as polygons or a suburb or postcode list
  • A start time, plus status updates when supply is restored
  • A refresh at least every 15 minutes, machine-readable (JSON or GeoJSON)
  • Public, or licensed for reuse with attribution

FAQ

Questions worth asking

Why not use the real battery figures?
Carriers treat battery endurance as commercial-in-confidence. Rather than invent per-site numbers, DarkVic carries one visible assumption (2, 4 or 8 hours) and shows how the answer moves with it.
Has it been validated against real blackouts?
Not yet. Carriers' outage data isn't public, so comparison against ground-truth observations is the natural next step. So far there are two episodes: a reconstructed replay of the February 2024 storms, showing what DarkVic would have inferred, and the live 3 October 2026 earthquake, where it recorded exposure but inferred no blackout because no mains-loss evidence followed.
Can it cover all of Victoria?
Yes, additively. Victoria has five electricity distributors and AusNet is piloted, so statewide coverage means four more pollers with the same rules, register and map.
Is this an operational emergency system?
No. It's an educational prototype. Inference never carries the authority of observation, and every verdict says "probable" for exactly that reason.
What does "no evidence" mean on the map?
Exactly that: uncertainty, not safety. Silence from a tower can mean it's fine or that it can no longer report, and DarkVic never paints silence as green.
